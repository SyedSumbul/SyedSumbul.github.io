Biography

I am a behavioural science researcher with an interdisciplinary training spanning economics, psychology, mental health, and behavioural science.

I finished my undergraduate degree in Economics. After that, I moved to Scotland and completed my first MSc in Psychology and Mental Health from the Univesity of Edinburgh. I worked in the UK for two years and then moved to the Netherlands for a Research Master’s in Behavioural Science from Radboud University. Currently, I am working as a Research Officer at Tilburg Schhol of Humanities and Digital Sciences, Tilburg University.

Alongside research, I have worked across scholarly publishing, open science, and science communication.

Research Lines

Behaviour, Food, and Sustainability

I am interested in how psychological, social, and political factors shape food-related decisions and environmentally significant behaviour. My Research Master’s project examined the relationship between political attitudes, climate concerns, and red meat consumption among wealthy Indians.

Computational and Quantitative Behavioural Research

I use quantitative and computational approaches to study behavioural processes across individual and population levels. My experience includes agent-based modelling, longitudinal and survey data, generalized linear models, mixed-effects models, structural equation modelling, and Bayesian modelling.

Open Science, Publishing, and Science Communication

I am interested in improving how research is produced, communicated, and made accessible. My work spans diamond open-access publishing, editorial workflows, research dissemination, public-facing science communication, and engagement with open and reproducible research practices.
